Rules and scope

This schedule covers personal information in live systems, operational records, support correspondence, processor copies, and backups. Retain only what is needed for a defined purpose. A listed maximum is not a reason to keep unnecessary information until that date. A valid deletion request may require earlier removal; a documented legal or dispute hold may require longer retention.

Periods below are measured from the stated trigger. Unless stated otherwise, cleanup must finish by the end of the period, including routine processing time. Account deletion is governed by the account-deletion policy.

Proposed record schedule

Routine support correspondence 12 months after case closure. Delete correspondence and attachments; move necessary privacy/security evidence into the appropriate restricted record first.
Privacy requests and deletion confirmations 24 months after closure. Temporary verification evidence: delete within 30 calendar days after verification unless needed for a documented dispute. Keep a minimal outcome/verification record, not copies of credentials or unnecessary identity documents.

Security and administrative audit records, including score-change reasons At most 24 months after event, unless an open investigation, legal minimum, or necessary replay record justifies a documented exception. Remove unnecessary identity/free text; delete expired records or retain only effectively anonymous evidence. Preserve enough explanation for retained league results.
Privacy-breach register Where PIPEDA applies: at least 24 months after the organization determines the breach occurred; proposed routine expiry at that point if no hold remains. Retain the minimal required record of every breach, including ones that do not require notification; delete after the minimum and any documented hold.
Draft/roster/transaction/lineup/standings history linked to fantasy managers Season duration plus 24 months after the season is finalized. Clear direct account references earlier on deletion and review identifying names/payloads within 30 calendar days after verification. Delete or effectively anonymize manager-linked history. Coordinate scoring inputs and derived outputs; keep no unnecessary manager identifier in cross-league aggregates.
Retry/idempotency command records Until 90 calendar days after the related draft, transaction, or league season is closed; earlier on account deletion where safe. Delete with a control that rejects stale retries and cannot recreate an expired action. Do not introduce arbitrary cleanup that compromises competition integrity.
Approved athlete records, statistics, source snapshots/imports, mappings, and scoring revisions Up to 5 years after the covered season ends, with annual purpose/rights review and any shorter source-licence limit. Delete unneeded personal/source data; coordinate derived results and replay inputs. Reject unused preview imports within 90 calendar days unless a documented review remains open. Athlete data is not automatically anonymous.
Effectively anonymous aggregate statistics While useful, with annual review. Retain only if individuals cannot reasonably be identified, including in small leagues; otherwise apply the relevant personal-data limit.
Backups, exports, replicas, and recovery copies Proposed maximum: 90 calendar days after information is removed from live systems. Temporary operational exports: delete within 30 calendar days after their task completes. Restrict recovery copies, expire them, and reapply deletions before a restore is made available. Confirm provider snapshots, point-in-time recovery, and exported copies all meet the limit.

There is no universal statutory retention period for ordinary fantasy-league or chat records. The operational periods above are proposals chosen for review against actual necessity, source rights, and costs. Purpose-based disposal follows the OPC's retention guidance.

The conditional breach-record minimum comes from Breach of Security Safeguards Regulations, section 6. It is measured from determination of the breach, rather than case closure.

Browser and device storage

These browser lifetimes are observed in the current repository, not evidence of Auth-provider database/log expiry. Renewing or using a session can set a fresh cookie.

Storage Current configured lifetime Purpose and user control
Access-session cookie Up to 1 hour, with a 60-second minimum cookie lifetime Authenticated requests; sign out or clear browser storage. Token validity is separately enforced.
Refresh-session cookie 30 days each time set Session renewal; sign out or clear browser storage.
Request-security cookie 1 day each time set Protect account actions from forged requests; cleared by sign-out flow.
Account-confirmation/recovery flow cookies 15 minutes Complete the authentication flow; clear browser storage.
Light/dark theme cookie 1 year each time set Remember appearance; change preference or clear browser storage.
Native app session storage, if distributed Until sign-out or app/device storage removal, subject to Auth session validity Maintain the session on the device. Confirm platform behavior before native release.

Account deletion does not guarantee removal of every cookie or independently stored local copy. The theme preference can remain until expiry or clearing.

Holds, disposal, and oversight

A legal, security, moderation, or privacy-access hold must identify its basis, relevant records, owner, start date, and review date. Review at least every 90 calendar days; restrict access and retain only the relevant subset. A pending access request requires preservation long enough for applicable access/complaint rights. When a hold ends, dispose of records already beyond their normal expiry promptly.

Removing an account reference, hashing a user identifier, or using a substitute name may be pseudonymization rather than anonymization. Treat linkable records, audit entity IDs, chat text, and team names as personal information until a documented assessment establishes otherwise.

Before publication, inventory every copy and processor, configure cleanup, test both ordinary and administrator deletion, and verify that retained source references do not block deletion. Maintain a minimal deletion/restriction record long enough to prevent restoration during the backup window. Record disposal results without copying the deleted content into the audit record.

Review this schedule at least annually and after significant feature, provider, legal, or processing changes. Material changes must also be reflected in the privacy and deletion policies.
