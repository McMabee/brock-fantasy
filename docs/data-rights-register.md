The operator proposes an athlete opt-out policy. Record the notice athletes receive, permitted purpose and fields, how requests reach Tarik, verification, response target, suppression of future imports, impact on historical competition records, and any permitted retention. Do not describe silence as an athlete's documented individual consent.

Privacy review must distinguish institutional/source permission, copyright, and the handling of athletes' personal information. The OPC's [meaningful-consent guidance](https://www.priv.gc.ca/en/privacy-topics/privacy-for-businesses/appropriate-handling-of-personal-information/collecting-personal-information-and-consent/consent/gl_omc_201805/) explains that the appropriate form of consent depends on context. Canada's [publicly available information regulations](https://laws.justice.gc.ca/eng/regulations/SOR-2001-7/page-1.html) specify particular categories; they are not a blanket exemption for everything visible online. Applicability and the proposed opt-out process remain for the operator's review.

For each approved import, record permission ID/reference, original CSV hash, normalization revision hash, official evidence hash, approver, approval date, season, allowed fields, display/attribution, retention, term/territory, and correction/revocation contact. The preview keeps these approvals pending and creates no active athletes or games.
